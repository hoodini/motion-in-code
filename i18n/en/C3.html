03

Icons and official logos

Thousands of free symbols, and what we're allowed to do with them.

Two kinds of symbols end up in almost every film. Icons are generic symbols: a dumbbell, a flame, a clock. Logos are company symbols: GitHub, React, Claude. Both have huge free libraries, and all of them are vector (SVG), so they stay sharp at any zoom.

03.1

Self-drawing icons

Lucidelucide-reactSVGISC

What you see

Eight fitness icons, each drawing itself stroke by stroke, a quarter of a second after the one before, and at the end they all light up in orange. Try the stroke width slider: same icons, a completely different character.

What Lucide is

An open-source icon library with nearly 2,000 icons, under the ISC license (everything is allowed, commercial use included). Every icon is drawn with strokes of the same thickness on a 24×24 grid, so they all look like one family. lucide-react gives us each icon as a React component: <Dumbbell />.

How it works

It's the same trick as the ring (01.4): we measure the length of each stroke (getTotalLength), turn it into a dashed stroke with a single dash of exactly that length, and slide that dash from "hidden" to "visible" based on t. An icon built from several strokes draws them one after another.

Shortcut: if we give each stroke pathLength="1", the browser treats every stroke as if its length were 1, so there's nothing to measure.

When to useFeature lists, steps, "what you get". Self-drawing pulls the eye to the next item.
When not toMixing two icon libraries in the same film: different stroke weights and styles look sloppy.

03.2

Official logos

Simple IconsSVGCC0 · trademarks apply

What you see

A wall of 16 logos from the tools that built this page, popping in one after another in their official brand colors. Try unchecking "Official brand colors" and checking "Dark background". The one-color version, in black or white, is usually the only version brands allow besides their official color, which is why the demo switches to it and not to some other color.

What Simple Icons is

A collection of more than 3,400 brand logos, each one a clean SVG, together with the brand's official color code. The simple-icons package on npm gives us both the shape and the color of every logo, so Claude doesn't have to draw a logo from memory, and won't invent a shape that's "roughly" right.

What's allowed and what isn't

The files themselves are CC0, public domain. But a logo is a trademark, and that's a different law entirely. Simple Icons itself asks everyone to respect each company's brand guidelines. In practice:

  • OK: showing a logo to say "works with" or "built with".
  • Not OK: changing it to an unofficial color, stretching it, cropping it or adding effects.
  • Not OK: implying that the company endorses us or is our partner.
  • The current official version of a logo lives on the company's Brand or Press page.
When to use"Works with your tools", "built with", clients who agreed to appear.
When not toClients who haven't agreed, or any situation where the logo looks like an endorsement we never got.

04

Kinetic typography

Text that moves with the music. Four techniques that replace almost every "fade".

Kinetic typography is text whose motion is part of the message: a word that lands hard feels important, a word that gets typed out feels like a conversation. In a launch video most of the screen is text, which makes this maybe the most important element in the catalog. The demo below runs four techniques back to back, two and a half seconds each.

04.1

Four techniques for moving text

Canvas 2DAntonRTL

SLAM: words that land

Each word appears on its own beat, starts too big and shrinks to its size with a spring, like a rubber stamp. Made for headlines of three or four words at the peak of the music.

MASK RISE: rising from a line

The words rise out of an invisible line, as if they were waiting just below it. The "line" is a mask: a rectangle that shows only what's inside it, like a stencil. The text moves and the mask doesn't, so the words look like they're born from the line.

POP TYPE: typing

Letter after letter, each one pops a little as it's typed, with a blinking cursor. The pace is uneven, like a real person typing, and every keystroke lands on a half beat or a quarter beat (what musicians call an "eighth note" and a "sixteenth note"; at 120 BPM those are 0.25 and 0.125 seconds), so the typing "plays" along with the music.

MARKER: highlighter

A strip of color stretches behind the important word, in the reading direction (left to right in English, right to left in Hebrew). The simplest way to tell the viewer "this is the word".

Right-to-left languages (Hebrew, Arabic): three things to ask forReveal words from right to left, in reading order. Wrap numbers and English words in unicode-bidi: isolate. Why? The browser lays out Hebrew right to left, and English and numbers left to right. Neutral characters like +, % and the space take their direction from their neighbors, so sometimes they wander to the other side. This setting tells the browser to lay out the wrapped piece as one sealed unit, without mixing it into the direction of the sentence. And give condensed display fonts more size: Anton is condensed (so is Karantina, our Hebrew headline font), so a headline in it can be set about 1.25× larger than one in a wider font and still carry the same visual weight.
When to useIn every launch video. SLAM for the peak, MASK RISE for sentences, POP TYPE for prompts and chats, MARKER for a key word.
When not toMore than two techniques in the same scene: the text starts competing for attention instead of telling a story.
