05

3D with three.js

Phones, glass, particles, ocean. And what every word in the prompt means.

three.js is the most popular library for 3D on the web, free under the MIT license. It sits on top of WebGL (the browser's way of drawing with the graphics card) and spares us the thousands of lines of code WebGL demands. To understand a 3D prompt, it helps to think of a film set:

  • Scene: the stage, where we place everything.
  • Camera: where we look from, and how wide the view is (wide like a wide-angle lens, or narrow like a telephoto, zoom-style lens).
  • Mesh (an object): every object in the scene. It is built from two parts: Geometry, the shape itself, and Material, the surface: its color, how shiny it is, how rough it is, whether it is transparent. The graphics card can draw one thing very fast, triangles, so every shape, even a sphere, is broken down into lots of small triangles.
  • Light, and the Environment map: a 360° image of the surroundings that shows up as reflections in shiny surfaces. Without it, metal and glass look gray and dead, because metal has almost no color of its own: what we see in it is whatever it reflects, and with no surroundings it reflects nothing.
  • Renderer (the photographer): turns all of this into one 2D image, the frame.

Two more terms keep coming up in the prompts. PBR (Physically Based Rendering) means materials that respond to light according to the laws of physics, so they look real under any lighting. We control them mostly with two numbers between 0 and 1: metalness (how metallic it is) and roughness (how rough it is: 0 is a mirror, 1 is chalk). GLTF is the standard file format for 3D models, "the JPEG of 3D": a model we built in a 3D program (for example Blender, which is free and appears at the end of this chapter) or downloaded from a site comes into three.js as a GLTF file. Drag any demo here to rotate it. That works only in the preview: in the film, the angle is computed from t.

05.1

Phone and laptop with live screens

three.jsCanvasTexturePBRMIT

What you see

A laptop lid opens, a phone rises beside it, and the camera slowly circles around them. The screens show live content: on the phone, a push-up counter fills up; on the laptop, code is being typed. Everything builds itself from simple shapes in code, with no model file at all.

An honest answer about "iPhone" and "MacBook"

These are generic devices inspired by a modern phone and laptop, not Apple models. Apple's device designs and its logo are legally protected. If we need a recognizable device, we use a GLTF model whose license allows that use, or the official device templates Apple publishes for marketers, following Apple's guidelines.

How it works

  • Each device's body is a box with rounded corners in a metallic PBR material, and it reflects a virtual "studio room" (RoomEnvironment).
  • The live screen: every frame, we draw the user interface on a regular 2D Canvas and turn it into a texture (CanvasTexture), a "sticker" stretched over the 3D screen. needsUpdate = true tells three.js to copy the new drawing to the graphics card. By default it does not copy, because copying an image to the graphics card every frame is expensive, and in most textures the image never changes.
  • The lid is a hinge: its rotation is computed from t with a spring. The camera moves on a circular path around the devices.
When to use"It works on every device", showing an app inside a 3D world, a transition from a flat interface to a real product.
When not toWhen the viewer needs to read what's on the screen: at a 3D angle, small text gets lost. Show the screen flat first, and only then put it into the device.

05.2

Breathing glass orb

three.jsMeshPhysicalMaterialshader

What you see

A glass orb that slowly changes shape, like a droplet, with a ring and small spheres circling it. Behind it sits the word YUV.AI, and the glass bends the text, exactly the way a glass of water distorts whatever is behind it.

The material: real glass

MeshPhysicalMaterial is the advanced material in three.js. Three settings turn it into glass: transmission (how much light passes through it), thickness (how thick it is, because thick glass bends light more), and ior, the index of refraction: how much light bends when it enters the material. For glass it's about 1.5, for water 1.33.

How it "breathes"

Every point on the orb's surface is pushed outward or inward according to noise: a function that gives random but smooth values, so neighboring points get similar values, and the result looks organic rather than jittery. The calculation is done by a shader: a small program that runs on the graphics card, on all the points in parallel. We inject a few lines into the built-in three.js shader with onBeforeCompile, and pass t to it as a variable (uTime).

When to useA "premium" moment, a logo or product name behind glass, a transition between chapters.
When not toOn a bright, flat background: the glass has nothing to refract, and it looks like gray plastic.
Full prompt

</build>

- After displacing vertices, recompute normals in the shader (or use a normal-from-noise approximation), or the lighting looks flat.
- For a seamless loop, make every periodic motion an integer number of cycles over the clip's length.
</gotchas>

05.3

20,000 particles that morph

three.jsPointsGPU

What you see

Twenty thousand points moving between four shapes: a sphere, a knot, the word AI, and a galaxy. The same technique runs at the top of this page. Wait: aren't "particles" on zero's banned list? They are, because aimless particles drifting in the background are a cliché. Particles that turn into a word and tell us something are a technique.

How it works

  • Points is a three.js object that draws every point in a list as a dot on screen, all of them at once on the graphics card. 20,000 dots as regular page elements (the DOM) would choke the browser (see lab 0.6).
  • For each shape we precompute 20,000 target positions. The word is sampled from a drawing of it: we write it on a 2D Canvas and read which pixels got painted.
  • Each point's position = a blend between the previous shape and the next one, following the progress p with easing: mix(A, B, ease(p)). Every point gets a small delay of its own, so the transition looks like a swarm and not like a solid block.
When to useAn opening or a closing, "from chaos to order", a logo built from thousands of points.
When not toAs a moving background behind text: that's exactly the cliché, and it competes with reading.
Full prompt

Particles are small soft dots in #FFC74E, #FF8F3A, #F56B49 and a little #5DE4E9, additive blending on #0B0E2A. The camera drifts slowly.
Banned: particles drifting randomly in the background, bloom halos around everything, a linear morph.

Custom point shader for round soft dots.
Capture with Playwright at 60fps, assemble with ffmpeg.
</build>

<gotchas>
- Every shape must have exactly the same particle count; resample, never truncate.
- Seed the random jitter (a fixed seed), so frame 300 is identical on every render.
- Additive blending with depthWrite on causes black squares; set depthWrite: false.
</gotchas>

05.4

Ocean and sky: from day to sunset

three.jsSkyWater

What you see

A calm sea and a sky that moves from midday to sunset and back, in twelve seconds. This is the time-lapse from the YUV.AI MOTION launch film, where the devices float above the water.

How it works

  • Sky is a three.js addon that computes the color of the sky from the sun's position, using a physical model of how light scatters in the atmosphere. Why orange? Air scatters blue light far more than red light. When the sun is low, its light passes through much more air on the way to us, most of the blue scatters off to the sides, and what reaches us is mostly orange and red. We don't paint a sunset: we lower the sun.
  • Water is an addon that draws the scene one more time, as if in a mirror under the surface of the water, and distorts that reflection with a wave pattern that moves according to t.
  • The time of day is a single number computed from t, and from it we derive the sun's height, the color of the light and the exposure (the overall brightness of the image).
When to use"Time passing", atmosphere, a backdrop for a 3D product scene, an emotional ending.
When not toA pure interface film: a whole world distracts from the app.
Full prompt

- Update the sun direction on both the Sky and the Water, or the reflection points at a different sun.
- Lower exposure at sunset, or the sky blows out to white.
</gotchas>

05.5

Card carousel that snaps to the beat

three.jssum of springs

What you see

Six cards on a ring in 3D. The ring turns one step on every beat, overshoots a little and settles. This is the moment in the launch film where the click bursts into a carousel.

How it works: a sum of springs

The ring's angle is a sum: every beat has its own spring, which starts at that beat's time and adds one step (60°, because 360° divided by 6 cards is 60°). angle = Σ step × spring(t − beat_i). The symbol Σ (sigma) means "add them all up": for every beat i, which starts at time beat_i, we add its spring. Each spring overshoots the target a little and comes back, which is why every step "lands". And since this is a sum of closed-form formulas, we can compute the angle at any moment, even if we jump straight to the middle.

Each card also rotates around its own axis so it always faces the camera. Otherwise the text on the cards at the back would read reversed, as in a mirror.

When to usePresenting categories, features or templates, where each one gets its own beat.
When not toWhen the content of the cards has to be read closely: they go by too fast.

05.6

3D equalizer

three.jsInstancedMesh

What you see

A 20×20 grid of bars with a wave running through it, and every beat gives it a "kick" from the center outward. The color depends on height: low bars are blue, medium ones orange, tall ones gold. Here the wave comes from a formula; in a real film the heights come from the music itself.

From the music, without breaking the clock

A live audio analyzer in the browser runs on the wall clock (real time, not the film's t), so it doesn't work for frame-by-frame capture. Instead, we analyze the song in advance: for every frame we compute the spectrum, how much energy there is in each frequency range (bass, mids, highs), and store it in a table. During capture we just read the row for t.

How it works: one shape, 400 instances

Every separate object in three.js is a separate drawing instruction that the processor (CPU) sends to the graphics card (a draw call). Thousands of these every frame slow the processor down, even when the graphics card has capacity to spare. This matters mostly for the live preview (lab 0.6); the film comes out smooth either way. InstancedMesh sends one instruction: "draw this box 400 times, here are the position and color of each one". Same image, a fraction of the work.

When to useA music video, "the music is the clock", an energetic build-up before the drop.
When not toA film without prominent music: an equalizer moving over silence looks disconnected.

When you need more than this

  • React Three Fiber: three.js in the form of React components (React is a popular library for building web interfaces). Useful when the scene is large and the project is already built in React. MIT. Version 9 is made for React 19.
  • Spline: a visual 3D editor in the browser, no code. We design by hand and export to GLTF, three.js or React. There is a free plan.
  • Blender: a full 3D program, free, under the GPL license: we may use it for any purpose, commercial included, and what we create with it is ours; the GPL terms apply only to someone who modifies the program itself and distributes it. We build custom models in it and export them as GLTF. A steep learning curve, results without limits.
